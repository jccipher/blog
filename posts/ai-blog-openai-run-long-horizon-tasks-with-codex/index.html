Editorial summary

OpenAI's GPT-5.3-Codex has demonstrated the ability to execute long-horizon tasks, such as building a design tool from scratch in a 25-hour session. The experiment involved running Codex with 'Extra High' reasoning, resulting in the generation of approximately 30,000 lines of code and the use of about 13 million tokens. This showcases Codex's improved capabilities in following instructions, staying on task, and self-correcting errors. The shift in focus from one-shot intelligence to extended autonomous work is emphasized, with practical applications in multi-step execution and mid-flight steering. The experiment also highlights the importance of durable project memory, where specifications, constraints, and status are stored in markdown files to prevent drift and maintain a stable definition of 'done.' The results suggest that long-horizon coding is moving toward less babysitting and more delegation with guardrails. However, the experiment was not a production rollout, and the model's performance is subject to limitations, including the need for structured context and the absence of general safety guarantees.
