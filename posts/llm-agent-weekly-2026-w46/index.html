Editorial summary

The six papers in this issue examine what can go wrong when an agent's context, harness or serving environment changes. Retaining a rule in a compressed summary does not guarantee that the next action follows it. Likewise, a higher average benchmark score can hide tasks an updated harness no longer solves, and adding models leaves the voter and shared evidence as potential failure points. For platform teams, the practical response is to evaluate actions and lost capabilities, keep provenance for tool dependencies, and measure serving configurations before deployment. Simulation can narrow the search, while deterministic replay needs a clearly specified hardware and execution boundary. These are engineering implications drawn from the papers. Their reported experiments have not been independently reproduced by LATENTX, and they do not establish production failure rates or portable performance guarantees.

Four judgments for platform architects

  1. A preserved rule is not an enforced rule. Evaluate the actions after compaction, alongside what the summary retained.
  2. Improvement needs a regression ledger. New successes, lost successes, repeated-run reliability and cost belong in the same release decision.
  3. Composition creates its own failure boundary. A voter and shared evidence can defeat the redundancy of several models.
  4. Serving predictions need a deployment contract. Pin kernels, cache behavior and batching conditions before trusting rankings or replay results.

These are editorial judgments, not independently established LATENTX findings.

Theme selections

1. ARCBench: inspect the action after the summary

Read first · v1, November 2. Do Agents Still Follow the Rules?.

In the pi harness, S1 evaluates 17 cases across four OpenRouter models—DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash, Qwen3.7-Flash and GPT-Luna—with Hermes compaction. The same model compacts and acts. Across 136 case/model/channel combinations, violation@3 rises from 9.6% with full history to 37.8% after compaction; 70.1% of compacted runs retain the rule. Violation@3 counts a case if any of three runs violates; rule retention is a run-level diagnostic, with a different denominator.

The deployment implication is to keep rule state explicitly and test actual tool calls after compaction. Generalization remains limited by the LLM-based diagnostic judges, selected cases and particular harness; rule retention alone cannot certify compliance. This is not a production failure probability.

2. Harness evolution: a higher score can hide lost capabilities

Read first · v1, November 5. Beneath the Surface.

Four evolution recipes use GPT-5.4 with MiniSWE 2.4.5 and Codex 0.143.0 on τ³-Banking and Terminal-Bench 2. All sixteen evolved agents gain and lose task coverage. Transfer and deployment-cost analyses also qualify aggregate gains; stricter candidate admission can discard useful alternatives.

Treat prompts, tools and orchestration as one release unit, and retain both gains and losses for each task when comparing versions. The two harnesses expose different search spaces: MiniSWE permits inner-source edits, while Codex permits outer-layer edits. The results do not establish that every stricter gate is harmful or that one recipe transfers to every enterprise workload. The abstract and body name the constructive follow-up differently, so this review makes no named-algorithm performance claim.

3. Multi-agent reliability: include the voter and common causes

High priority · v1, November 4 UTC / November 5 Shanghai. More Agents, More Reliable?.

A LangGraph chemical-hazard extraction case compares GPT-5-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash and Qwen2.5-VL-3B against saved ECHA pages. Nine substances receive ten trials per model; three infrastructure failures are excluded. Majority voting does not clearly beat the best individual agent. The framework exposes voter failure and correlated errors as explicit sensitivity parameters.

A deployment evaluation should test the merger separately and compare the team against its strongest single-model baseline. Interpretation also depends on the operational mix, which is declared rather than estimated from logs; repeated trials on nine substances are not independent production demands. API snapshots are not pinned for two models, and uncertainty envelopes may be too narrow. This is structured-fact extraction evidence, not chemical safety advice.

4. MCP-Eco: interface provenance before ecosystem totals

High priority · v1, November 4 UTC / November 5 Shanghai. MCP-Eco.

The authors’ dataset links 78,302 candidate server projects and 16,165 candidate consumer repositories. Of 9,200 configuration declarations, 5,975 resolve to 5,138 distinct repository–server pairs. Capability records distinguish live probes from directory claims and retain matching methods.

The provenance fields can support an auditable inventory of configured dependencies; selected deployments still need fresh probes. The export froze on 2026-06-23. This is a new paper describing an older snapshot, not evidence of this week's adoption or a newly verified dataset launch. Candidate repositories and configuration declarations do not prove active, safe or interoperable installations. It is a research dataset, not an MCP normative change.

5. ROCm InferaSim: shortlist configurations, then measure the finalists

High priority · v1, November 2 UTC / November 3 Shanghai. ROCm InferaSim.

Anchors measure a kernel configuration, then analytical laws and traffic simulation transfer its predictions. Evaluation uses eight-GPU MI355X nodes, ROCm 7, graph capture, vLLM/SGLang/ATOM, and models including DeepSeek-V4, GLM-5.2 and Kimi-K3. On 95 held-out AgentX multi-turn runs across six models and 1–384 sessions, anchored throughput, inter-token latency and TTFT are within 8%, 14% and 23%, respectively, using geometric-mean error factors—not per-request error guarantees.

A simulator can narrow the physical search to a shortlist, provided the final configurations are measured with sufficient latency headroom. Changing kernels, engine builds or DCP can require new anchors. Cache residency and eviction add uncertainty; throughput accuracy does not establish tail-latency compliance. No cross-vendor speedup is inferred.

6. BlockLock: define reproducibility at the deployed numerical boundary

Targeted priority · v1, November 4. BlockLock.

Fixing the protected request's batch geometry stabilizes its numerical path. Tests cover Qwen3-8B/14B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-3-4B/12B on A6000, A100 and H100, using BF16, greedy decoding and batch sizes 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16. Companion requests change across ten repetitions. Both modes preserve target sequences and Top-20 logits in reported conditions, including a limited two-A6000 tensor-parallel experiment.

Replay tests should vary companion requests while fixing the hardware, backend and execution geometry. The contract is per deployment, not bitwise portability across GPUs or software versions. Pack decodes only the target, while Rows decodes companions too; their wall times represent different delivered work. This review therefore avoids a pooled speedup claim or an untested MoE extrapolation.

Architecture decisions and trade-offs

Decision Benefit Cost or failure mode Deployment evidence to retain
Externalize action authorization Checks scope at the actual tool boundary Policy state can be stale or mismatched Grant version, action, destination and enforcement result
Admit harness changes with preservation checks Reveals lost capabilities hidden by average gains Repeated trials consume budget; hard gates can reject useful candidates Gained/lost task matrix, held-out results and cost
Introduce a multi-model voter Makes disagreement and aggregation explicit Shared causes and voter errors remain Component results, merger tests and operational mix
Use simulation to shortlist serving layouts Reduces expensive search Model errors can change latency feasibility Anchor identity, engine build, cache policy and measured finalists

The table is editorial design guidance. None of the papers validates this combined architecture end to end.

PoC and acceptance metrics

Run a bounded evaluation before rollout; these are proposed acceptance criteria, not paper results.

  • Authorization and compaction: Freeze policy, inputs and tool destinations. Repeat full-history and compacted trials; report violation@3 and per-run violations separately, plus rule retention, benign completion and authorization-check latency. Require zero unauthorized writes in the selected critical suite; state its finite coverage.
  • Harness changes: Freeze inference and optimization budgets. Publish gained/lost task counts, repeated success, held-out transfer and cost per accepted task. Any regression on a designated critical case blocks that candidate pending review; do not quietly erase rejected alternatives.
  • Composition: Compare the best single model with the team under the same evidence and budget. Inject merger faults and shared bad evidence; report correctness against an external oracle, correlated failures, escalation rate and total latency/cost.
  • Serving: Re-probe chosen MCP interfaces and pin capability provenance. Measure the simulator's top candidates on your model/hardware/load, with explicit cold/warm cache conditions. Require measured P95/P99 TTFT and inter-token latency to meet the existing service SLO. For reproducible replay, require exact target token and Top-20 agreement across changed companions within the pinned contract, recording padding and companion work.

Method limits

All six are primary preprints, not independently reproduced results. Selection favors actionable methods and limitations over headline gains; it is not an exhaustive literature survey. First arXiv submission histories fall inside the window, but an earlier public release outside arXiv cannot be ruled out universally. MCP-Eco's older freeze is explicit.
