> A successful pilot is a protected experiment; production begins only when ownership, quality, cost, oversight, and transition decisions survive ordinary organizational conditions.

## Editorial summary

Anthropic and Accenture argue that pilots are structurally easier than production. They use selected teams, bounded scope, protected budgets, and clear timelines, while enterprise deployment must operate amid shared ownership, uneven adoption, changing data, and normal budget pressure.

The guide reports that 23% of surveyed C-suite leaders had sustained enterprise-wide AI impact and that 42% of organizations split accountability between IT and finance without one owner for cost and outcomes. The proposed response is a sequence of seven decisions spanning before, during, and after the pilot.

Two frameworks make the advice concrete. Define the job through user, task, output, and a measurable quality threshold, then estimate total cost of ownership before the pilot. Match oversight to risk with automated, sampled, reviewed, and advisory tiers instead of applying one review policy to every output.

The blueprint is useful because it moves the go/no-go decision upstream. Its survey figures and recommendations come from the publishers' research and deployment experience, so organizations should validate assumptions locally. A pilot should not graduate because the demo worked; it should graduate when accountable owners can operate the system under production constraints.
